1. Start with evidence, not animation alone

This laboratory connects a selected physical part, its engineering purpose, a governing equation and an observable model response. The learning aim is not simply to make parts move. A learner should predict a response, make a controlled change, record the result and explain discrepancies using the stated assumptions.

Reference, not manufacturer accuracy. The assemblies are original teaching geometry, not photorealistic OEM CAD. All readings are generated locally by an averaged numerical model. The magnetic overlay is not FEA. The software is not connected to the professor’s retrofit or to a physical battery, motor or CAN bus.

Open index.html in a current desktop browser. Begin with the complete vehicle, then choose Motor & magnetic field. Set Explode near 60%, start the driving demo, switch on Magnetic field and open Instruments → Phase currents. The field control enables X-ray. Close the inspector when it obscures the scene. Pause and use +0.1 s to explain the sequence without time pressure.

Student prerequisites and safety

Students should know DC voltage/current/power, three-phase fundamentals and torque/angular speed. Use the worked examples to bridge gaps. This is a screen-based exercise. Do not translate virtual probe locations, contactor timings or disassembly animations into a procedure on live hardware. Real high-voltage work requires the relevant authorised procedures and qualified supervision.

3. Twelve worked examples

All numerical values below use the declared reference configuration. The general motor and wheel relations are linked in the primary sources; the parameter choices are project assumptions.

Example 1. Pack and module energy

Vpack = 96 × 3.2 = 307.2 V Epack = 307.2 × 200 / 1000 = 61.44 kWh Vmodule = 8 × 3.2 = 25.6 V Emodule = 25.6 × 200 / 1000 = 5.12 kWh

Series connection increases voltage, not the Ah rating. Twelve 8s modules give 96 series cells. Nominal energy is not the same as available energy at every current, temperature and state of health.

Example 2. Pre-charge and stored energy

τ = RC = 100 × 0.002 = 0.200 s t95 = −RC ln(0.05) = 0.599 s Iinitial = V/R = 307.2 / 100 = 3.072 A EC = ½CV² = ½ × 0.002 × 307.2² = 94.37 J

The numerical model uses the present battery voltage, not always the nominal 307.2 V. Separate wake/check delays from the RC charging time. These example values are not contactor-selection or discharge-safety instructions.

Example 3. Synchronous speed and electrical frequency

nmechanical = 60 f / p = 60 × 200 / 4 = 3000 rpm ωmechanical = 2π × 3000 / 60 = 314.159 rad/s

Here p = 4 pole pairs, equivalent to eight poles. An incorrect factor of two is a common error. In the PMSM overlay electrical angle advances four times mechanical angle.

Example 4. PMSM torque and q-axis current

Te = (3/2) p λm iq iq = 100 / (1.5 × 4 × 0.15) = 111.11 A Pmechanical = 100 × 314.159 = 31.416 kW

The surface-PM teaching relation uses zero d-axis current and peak-valued current conventions. It is not a complete field-weakening controller. Mechanical output differs from battery input because of losses.

Example 5. Induction-machine comparison

s = (ns − nr) / ns nr = (1 − 0.03) × 3000 = 2910 rpm

The 3% slip is prescribed in this app to teach relative motion. It is not predicted from rotor resistance, equivalent-circuit parameters or load. The comparison must not be interpreted as a validated induction-motor drive simulation.

Example 6. Gear reduction and vehicle speed

G = 3 × 3 = 9 nwheel = 3000 / 9 = 333.333 rpm v = (3000 × 2π/60) × 0.349 / 9 = 12.182 m/s v = 43.857 km/h Twheel = 100 × 9 × 0.97 = 873 N·m

The wheel relation assumes no longitudinal tyre slip. The internal differential bevel gearset and lateral cornering are not solved. Use signed quantities for reversing, but distinguish direction from power consumption.

Example 7. Road-load estimate at 20 m/s on level ground

Frolling = 1850 × 9.81 × 0.010 = 181.485 N Faero = ½ × 1.225 × 0.27 × 2.30 × 20² = 152.145 N Pwheel = (181.485 + 152.145) × 20 = 6.673 kW

This is a steady, level-road estimate before motor/inverter/gear losses and auxiliaries. Acceleration and gradient add terms. The model uses an assumed constant drag coefficient and rolling-resistance coefficient.

Example 8. DC/DC power balance

Iout = 400 / 13.8 = 28.99 A Pin = 400 / 0.93 = 430.11 W Loss = 30.11 W Iin at 307.2 V = 430.11 / 307.2 = 1.400 A

A lower output voltage does not imply lower output current. Do not compare HV and LV currents without considering power and efficiency.

Example 9. Charging path and net power

AC input = 7.2 kW OBC DC output = 7.2 × 0.94 = 6.768 kW Reference auxiliary HV load = 400 / 0.93 = 0.430 kW Net terminal charging power ≈ 6.338 kW before high-SoC taper

External DC charging bypasses the OBC in the reference path and uses a separate 50 kW limit. The application does not implement the charging-station protocol or communications handshake.

Example 10. Battery current under load

P = (Voc − IR)I I = 2P / [Voc + √(Voc² − 4RP)] For Voc = 320 V, R = 0.06 Ω, P = 30 kW: I = 95.459 A; Vterminal = 314.272 V; I²R = 546.740 W

These values are calculated from the stated example rather than taken from a measured cell. Terminal electrical load and internal battery heating must not be counted twice.

Example 11. Indicative range quotient

Usable energy = 61.44 × 0.90 = 55.296 kWh Range = 55.296 × 1000 / 185 = 298.897 km

This is an energy-to-consumption quotient, not a WLTP/EPA result or a prediction for the real retrofit. Changing the assumed Wh/km changes the answer immediately.

Example 12. Thermal interpretation

Cth dT/dt = Ploss − h(T − Tcoolant) With Cth,motor = 32000 J/K and 1000 W net heat: dT/dt = 0.03125 K/s before the coupled coolant changes

A lumped temperature is a component average. A coloured housing does not establish the internal winding hotspot, semiconductor junction temperature or a validated cooling design.

  2. The reduction gearbox
  3. The DC/DC converter
Instructor answer: B. The mechanical reduction lowers angular speed while multiplying torque, subject to losses.

Common misconceptions to address

READY is not a motion command. A battery is not a fixed-voltage source at every load. A DC/DC converter is not the traction inverter. An OBC is not in the external-DC charging power path. Regeneration is limited by operating state, speed, SoC and the reference torque/power envelope. A colour or moving marker is not a calibrated field/temperature measurement. A virtual fault is not a trained diagnosis. A visually detailed mesh is not evidence of manufacturer dimensions.

6. Component reference for the lecturer

The following entries are the editable teaching content used by the app. Construction statements describe this reference model; failure discussions explain concepts rather than provide workshop procedures.

A coupled energy-conversion system

Function. The battery stores chemical energy. Power electronics control electrical transfer; the machine converts between electrical and mechanical power; the reduction drive delivers torque to the wheels.

Construction. This original reference sedan has 96 series LFP cells, a rear electric drive and a 9:1 two-stage reduction. Its geometry is illustrative, not Renault, Tesla or another manufacturer’s CAD.

Operation. POWER wakes the low-voltage controller and executes pre-charge. READY is permission to drive, not a torque command. Select D and apply the accelerator, or run the explicit driving demonstration.

Observe. Watch the state, DC-link voltage, motor torque and wheel speed together. Changing the road gradient changes the required tractive force.

Diagnostic concept. An EV can have a healthy battery and still be unable to drive. Interlocks, current paths, sensing and controller permissions are separate diagnostic questions.

Traction battery pack

Function. A series connection raises voltage; a parallel connection raises charge capacity. In this reference pack, every cell contributes to the same series current.

Construction. Twelve independently explorable 8s modules form 96s1p. Rated reference values are 3.2 V per cell and 200 Ah: 307.2 V nominal and 61.44 kWh nominal. A cold plate, contactors, fuse, service disconnect and BMS complete the assembly.

7. Model and teaching boundaries

The numerical core advances a longitudinal reference vehicle, a battery with an assumed OCV/SoC relationship and series resistance, an averaged torque/power envelope, fixed conversion efficiencies and lumped thermal states. It is deterministic and auditable. That transparency is useful for teaching, but it is not vehicle or component validation.

Magnetic-field arrows show the spatial factor cos(pθ − θe) with p = 4. The diagram overlays geometry so it remains visible in an exploded view. It has no calibrated tesla scale and is not a finite-element result. Phase currents are balanced analytical references; PWM is a synthetic 12× carrier illustration. Semiconductor pulses and winding glow are slowed teaching cues, not the plotted real-time transistor switching state. The permanent-magnet field is conceptually retained when power is off; the rotating stator excitation requires torque permission.

Cooling uses average battery, motor, inverter and coolant temperatures. The pump and radiator illustrate system relationships; internal pump hydraulics and local temperature distributions are not modelled. The reduction drive preserves two 3:1 stages, but differential bevel gearing, road-contact forces, ABS, suspension dynamics and steering/cornering are not calculated.

The cell stack has enlarged layers. Conductive traces and busbars are educational geometry, not production-ready fabrication files. Series links inside the module follow the reference connection order; full pack harness engineering, insulation clearances, cell manufacturing details and terminal-material choices require a specific validated design. The charge modes do not implement a vehicle/EVSE communication protocol. No CAN analyser or insulation tester is connected.

The separate GLB files are editable standard assets with named nodes. They contain these same original reference shapes, not third-party manufacturer geometry. The app embeds baked copies for offline loading. A change to a GLB alone does not modify the app: the authoring pipeline or loader would need to incorporate it.

Software evidence versus scientific evidence

Automated checks establish that named controls and equations behave as implemented. They do not establish that the assumptions reproduce a real EV, that a learner achieves a particular outcome or that the software is safe for real maintenance. The test report states the browser, viewport and rendering environment. Physical iPhones, Android devices and Safari were not tested in this delivery.
